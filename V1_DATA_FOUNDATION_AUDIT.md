# StayGrubby V1 Data Foundation Audit

November 7, 2026 · Investigation at `3103fbd` · Decision input for the next architecture checkpoint.

Ship V1 without a new intelligence schema or event stream. Preserve the facts users already create. Before release, verify and close the narrow lifecycle gaps around exact Recipe-used provenance and links to deleted cooks. The only time-sensitive candidate for new history is the explicit reaction chosen at each completed re-rank. Add it before V1 only if the owner commits to dated private taste history and accepts the retention contract.

## November 8 follow-up

The inventory and initial recommendations below describe the November 7 checkpoint. The follow-up adopts private dated reactions for future completed sessions and closes two confirmed Recipe lifecycle gaps in the branch.

- **Exact use.** Cook sync completes the existing reservation on an exact owner and ranked-item match. The archived content, revision and creator credit come from that reservation. The additive migration repairs existing provable matches. Its repair timestamp marks reconciliation because the original completion time is unknown. Cooks without a matching reservation retain unknown Recipe use.
- **Current association.** The migration retires exact links pointing at tombstoned cooks. A new authorized Recipe-origin cook may fill a vacant exact link. Recovery of older reservations leaves association choice to the owner. The previous completion RPC acknowledges an already recorded cook so a journal replay cannot recreate an unlinked association. Legacy links to active Dish families still serve as associations, and ordinary New Version has no implicit Recipe origin.
- **Dated reaction.** The existing ranked-item diagnostics retain one event ID, completion time, reaction and initial/re-rank kind per completed session. Reaction-only finishes count. Cancellation and undone answers create no history. Earlier initial/latest values are preserved as undated evidence. The events travel with private cook sync and are absent from shared projections and active history after cook deletion. Account erasure follows the existing account boundary.
- **First-party evidence.** Derive future private summaries from retained cook dates, versions, explicit reactions, comparisons and verified Recipe-use snapshots. Recipe methods, authored times and ingredients describe instructions. Actual execution, proficiency and ingredient-specific preference require their own evidence.
- **Development persona.** Maya Ortiz uses the existing Debug-only library and social review harness. It includes linked and standalone Recipes, an exact revision-1 cook with current revision 2, a sibling ordinary version, multiple courses and reactions, unlocked ranking, posts, comments, Hands, Tickets and Sous Chef relationships. See the [launch instructions](DevelopmentTools/README.md#preloaded-development-persona).

**Architecture check.** A/B. Reuse cooking identities, existing reservations, owner sync and isolated fixtures. C/D. Capture a compact reaction per deliberate completion and reuse the existing diagnostics blob. E. Keep private evidence behind the account boundary, with the existing deletion and receipt rules. F. Live reconciliation uses the existing unique owner/item key and bounded lookups. The one-time repair joins only matching active cooks; check affected counts and preserved content/revisions before authorized deployment. Reaction storage grows with completed sessions. G. Verify restore, retry, deletion and owner isolation in the disposable backend harness and exercise the launch path in Debug and Release.

**Still open.** Recipe reservation expiry and snapshot retention after explicit cook deletion need a separate privacy decision. The migration leaves deleted snapshots outside active readers and preserves existing retention. Unified Dish/Recipe product design, default Recipe selection and any future intelligence experience remain separate work.

Deleting a cook removes its active rank entry and incident comparisons and retires its social post. Deleting the last cook also removes the Dish. Hosted tombstones, operation hashes/receipts and local sync payloads support consistency and recovery. Recipe deletion removes its revision chain and current references. Independent copies and completed Recipe-used snapshots on surviving cooks may remain with null source references. Exclude hidden records and abandoned reservations from active history.

## Dish and Recipe as one experience

- **Food identity / Dish** owns the durable food name, default classification, inspiration, descriptions and grouping of attempts. One Dish can have many cooks. An owner can also have a standalone Recipe with zero cooks. Compose a single authorized food view from those identities before considering a new root entity. Retain IDs and separate deletion semantics.
- **Recipe / revision** owns the authored instructions, ingredients/quantities, yield, durations, Recipe-specific metadata and photo, audience and attribution. Keep both current instructions and exact revision identity. Copying, attaching a revision to a post and cooking from a revision have different semantics. Never reconcile by title, photograph or fuzzy matching.
- **Attempt** owns what the user recorded about one cook, including date, sequence, actual version title, notes, Course/tags, photos and any explicitly chosen Recipe revision. A re-rank evaluates the same attempt. Keep ranking evidence, exact-used snapshot, author/creator credit, social publication and access decisions separate.
- One current exact link per Recipe and per cook **within the viewer's account** represents a selected association. Legacy family links coexist, and multiple attempts can retain Recipe-used snapshots. Independent names, Cuisine and Course can disagree. Display fallbacks must leave their sources intact. `Rank My Version` can reuse a linked Dish and reserves an exact revision. Ordinary **New Version** passes the Dish without a Recipe origin. Automatically treating it as a Recipe cook would fabricate evidence. A soft-deleted attempt can still occupy the exact link and block automatic association of a later cook. The current Recipe card filters out the deleted attempt's Dish but may still return its attempt ID.

## Post-V1 use of existing evidence

- **Cooking History:** Start with owner-only dates, retained versions, exact-use snapshots and editable notes. Show unknown dates as unknown. If a later “as cooked” view actually needs historical Cuisine or Source, capture the smallest owner-confirmed value at cook time going forward. Earlier values are unreconstructable. Avoid copying mutable notes, photographs or entire records for that purpose.
- **Taste Preferences and private Flavor profile:** Initial/latest reactions and observed choices are useful at the whole-cook level. Ingredient/technique patterns are interpretations with uncertainty. A high rank is not preference for each ingredient, and frequency is not a favorite. Use versioned, explainable, correctable inference over authorized data when the product exists. No stored inferred profile is needed now.
- **Cooking Interests:** A Saved Recipe is an owner-private, revocable bookmark to a creator's original. Want to Cook is a separate explicit intention, optionally resolved to a real cook later. Neither can be reconstructed from copying, opening or cooking. The dormant `recipe_tickets` owner/Recipe/time relation might fit one purpose after a contract review. A Collection or Cooking Series adds explicit many-to-many Dish context that frequency cannot infer. Store memberships only when users can create them. No duplicate food or evidence.
- **Cooking Ability and private Ability profile:** Cook counts and used recipes show exposure, not proficiency. Methods and authored times are not measured technique or ability. Begin with private summaries of experience. Any later self-report, explicit skill feedback or structured technique tags need a specific user benefit and correction controls. No public skill score.
- **Social Intelligence, discovery and recommendations:** Use deliberate shares, completed independent origin cooks and currently eligible social interactions as separate observations. Do not promote abandoned reservations, current links, forks, Hands or notifications to “Made X times.” Apply eligibility, blocks, moderation, revocation and deletion before selecting or aggregating candidates. Begin with on-demand, bounded queries once the product exists. No global ranking changes, popularity-as-quality or premature materialized aggregates.

Exclude impression/click/search streams, deleted text or removed relationships for personalization, tap-by-tap ranking logs, duplicate full snapshots for analytics, photo-derived flavor or skill, inferred allergies/religious compliance, a canonical global ingredient or Dish taxonomy, event warehouses, embeddings, external AI profiling, and recommendation infrastructure. Explicit dietary choices and ingredient-based Contains/Possible metadata are different signals. Neither constitutes a medical guarantee or permission to disclose dietary information.

## Privacy, migration and owner decisions

- Read-only hosted metadata matched **41/41 migration versions and hashes**. All **41 `staygrubby` tables** had RLS enabled. The catalog showed authenticated SELECT on 15 foundation tables, no direct client INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE grants there, no anonymous table grants in that schema and no anonymous `api` function EXECUTE. Spot-checked owner policies on Dishes, attempts and comparisons. Recipe/provenance/social tables inspected have no direct client grants; actor-derived RPCs and dedicated checks control projection. All four Storage buckets were private. Catalog inspection establishes configuration, not an adversarial authorization test.
- Future intelligence belongs behind the permanent account boundary, with no implicit access through My Kitchen, shared links, social payloads, counts or analytics. New owner-authored Recipes currently default to Sous Chefs through a visible control. A default audience supplies no consent for profiling or for exposing private cooking. Keep Private Notes, taste, ability and sensitive tags out of social and public projections. Disclose new processing purposes and give users inspection, correction and deletion controls before materializing inferred profiles. Validate pairwise isolation, blocks, revocation, account deletion and aggregate leakage before any shareable statistics. [Supabase RLS reference](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security)
- **Migration implications:** None for this audit. For later fixes, preserve existing IDs, comparison/assessment assignments, revisions, sync receipts and backward compatibility. Prefer additive nullable fields only where a verified contract needs one. Test two-account access, old clients, replay, restore, delete and revocation in fixtures. No guessed backfill or repurposing sync tombstones as behavioral history.
- **Owner decisions:** Is dated re-rank reaction history worth recording before launch? When a Dish has multiple related Recipes, should one be an explicitly selected default or should the cook choose each time? What should happen to a Recipe-only food when its first cook is added, or when its last cook or Recipe is deleted? What snapshot lifetime and creator credit should remain after deletion, and how should private intelligence controls be exposed later?
